# Scattered: The Researcher's Journal

## PRE-JOURNEY ENTRY
*Three days before departure*

---

I'm leaving tomorrow. (Am I? I've packed twice. Unpacked once. Now repacking.) 

The thing about looking for someone is that you have to believe they're still there to find. And I'm not sure I believe that anymore. About him, I mean. (Do I believe anything anymore?)

His last message was cryptic. "The lake remembers what we forget." Typical of him—always needed to sound like he was saying something profound when he was probably just tired. Or lost. Or both.

I've read about the area. Lutvann. The name keeps appearing in his research scattered across his desk like breadcrumbs. Except breadcrumbs lead you home and these... these just circle back. Always back to the same question: where did he go?

I'm not a detective. I'm not brave. I'm just someone who can't stop looking because stopping would mean accepting that he chose to disappear. And I can't—(I won't?)—believe that yet.

The drive is long. The bus goes to a certain point and then you have to walk. He walked it. He must have. All the notes reference the forest, the road that ends, the place where civilization stops.

Tomorrow I become the person looking for the person looking for me.

(But he wasn't looking for me, was he?)

---

## FIRST DAY: ARRIVAL AT THE ROADBLOCK
*Journal entry — Evening, Day 1*

---

The bus ride took longer than expected. Or maybe time moves differently when you're traveling toward something you're afraid of finding.

The driver dropped me at what he called "the end." He wasn't joking. There's a barrier. A real one. Metal gate. Official-looking. A sign that says ROAD CLOSED—FOREST PRESERVE. No one goes beyond. Something about erosion. Environmental protection. (Or maybe people kept disappearing and they had to do something official about it.)

I stood there for maybe twenty minutes. Just looking.

The forest beyond the gate is dense. Dark green bleeding into darker green. You can't see more than fifty feet in before the trees swallow the light. And somewhere in there—somewhere in that thickness—he was. He is. (Present tense or past tense? I don't know anymore.)

The gate isn't locked. That's the thing that got me. Everyone keeps saying the area is closed but the gate swings open. Easy. Like it's inviting. Or like whoever closed it gave up on keeping people out.

I climbed over.

(I actually did it. I'm in the forest now. I'm writing this by a fallen log, maybe a hundred meters past the barrier, and the sounds are all wrong. Too much rustling. Too much silence beneath the rustling. The light is filtered green. My hands are shaking. Either from cold or fear or adrenaline or all of it.)

The road continues past the gate. It's barely a road anymore—more like a path that remembers being a road. Worn flat by feet or time or both. And the notes. Christ, the notes start appearing immediately.

The first one was pinned to a tree at about shoulder height. Just a piece of paper. Weathered. The handwriting is his—I'm sure of it. But the words...

"You're looking in the wrong direction."

That's all it says. 

I stood there reading it three times. Four times. What does that even mean? Wrong direction toward what? Toward him? Toward the truth? Toward the lake? Toward myself?

(He always did this. Always left breadcrumbs that led nowhere. Always made you feel stupid for following them.)

I kept walking. The path descends now. The forest gets denser. And there are more notes. Scattered along the way like he—like *someone*—wanted to mark a trail. Or mark a descent. Or...

I can't think clearly. Everything smells like wet earth and rot and something else. Something old. The sound of water is getting closer. The lake, maybe. Or a stream. I can't tell.

I've made camp maybe a kilometer in. There's a small clearing. The light is fading fast—faster than it should. The trees block the sky entirely now.

I found another note near the clearing. This one is different. The handwriting is shakier. Almost mine. Almost. And it says:

"Turn back. You're becoming him."

I have no idea what that means.

(But I'm terrified it's true.)

---

*Observations:*
- The forest is alive in a way that's unsettling. Not dangerous. Just... aware.
- The notes are appearing too deliberately to be random. Someone is placing them. Or he is. Or...
- My hands won't stop shaking.
- I can hear water but I can't see it.
- The deeper I go, the less certain I am of anything. My own thoughts feel unreliable. Like they're being whispered to me instead of thought by me.
- Tomorrow I find the lake. 
- (Or it finds me.)

---

*End Day 1*
