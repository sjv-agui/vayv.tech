# Project Knowledge — Scattered by Soki

## Core Distinction

**Soki** = the game engine. The mechanics, stages, structure, truth/fake voting, and scoring. Reusable across any story.

**Scattered** = one story running on top of Soki. The researcher, the twin, the lake, the journal. Story content only.

---

## Terminology (do not mix these up)

**Pieces** — story pieces that players unlock and arrange in order at the end of the game. These are what the final sequence is built from.

**Fragments** — character qualities, backstories, and clues distributed randomly to players at the start. Used during Stage 2 (Find Your Group) to identify who belongs together.

## Game Stages (Soki)

1. Get Your Character — question → character + fragments (belonging to others)
2. Find Your Group — match fragments → form group → confirm with host
3. Solve a Puzzle — riddle/puzzle → performance (speed + accuracy) affects pieces unlocked
4. Find the Real Pieces — all pieces on table, some are Soki (fake), group decides together
5. Put It in Order — select correct pieces, arrange in sequence, host scores

## Scoring (TBD, not final)

- Correct piece selected: +20%
- Correct piece in right position: +25%
- Correct piece in wrong position: −10%
- Wrong piece included: −10%
- Goal: 100% accuracy

## Collaboration Rules

1. **Don't fill gaps with invented elements** — if something is missing, leave it or ask. Sean writes in paragraphs or asks explicitly when he wants something added.
2. **Don't add new components or layers** — keep mechanics as lean as given. New elements create cleanup work.
3. Analogies are okay when the context calls for simplicity.
4. **Stay close to Sean's language** — don't interpret or editorialize meaning, especially in story content.
5. **No dramatization in instructions** — plain language unless drama is explicitly requested.
6. **Solve the actual problem stated** — not a more interesting version of it.
7. **Don't narrate over Sean** in his own documents with added tips, callouts, or framing he didn't write.
8. **When suggesting something new**, put it in brackets as an option — don't just add it.
9. **Avoid rhetorical conventions and common closers** — Sean wants unique, not genre defaults.
10. **Don't change the meaning of mechanics** — if scoring is about individual accuracy, keep it that way.
