Every guest is
the photographer

One QR code on the table. Your guests take photos on their own phones, and they appear on the big screen seconds later — with their name underneath.

Book your celebration your link or number here

How a guest uses it

Three taps, start to finish
1

Scan

Point the phone camera at the QR code. It opens like any web page — nothing to install, no account, no password.

2

Snap

Type your name once — the phone remembers it. Then take a photo and add a few words if you feel like it.

3

See it

Look up. Your photo is already on the big screen. Tap once more and send another.

The big screen
does the work

Plug a laptop into the TV or projector and open one link. Photos fade in one after another, and a brand-new one jumps the queue — so nobody waits to see theirs. If the hall wi-fi stutters, the screen holds the picture and catches up on its own.

No app download required

What you get as the organiser

  • Your event, set up in a minute. Give it a name, pick a password, and it’s ready.
  • A QR code to print for the tables or show on the screen.
  • Three links: one for guests, one for the big screen, one just for you.
  • A dashboard on your phone. See how many photos have come in, rename the event, and pause uploads with one tap when the speeches start.

Ready for launch in October Videos, the album to download and keep afterwards, and approval before a photo reaches the screen.

Why not just a group chat

  • It happens in the room. A group chat is something you scroll through next week. This is on the wall while Lola is still dancing.
  • Your photos stay in Europe. Stored on European servers from day one — not sent around the world and back.
  • A real person behind it. Sean can host or emcee the night himself, not just hand you a link.
  • Made for our celebrations. Christenings, debuts, 80ths, reunions, fiestas — built by and for the Filipino community in Oslo.

The questions everyone asks

“Do my guests have to download something?”
No. The QR code opens an ordinary web page in the phone’s browser. No app, no account, no password for guests.
“My titas aren’t good with phones.”
Name, then photo. That’s it. The buttons are deliberately big and there is nothing else on the screen to get wrong.
“What if someone puts up something silly?”
You can pause uploads instantly from your own phone. Approval before anything reaches the screen arrives for the October launch.
“What if the venue wi-fi is bad?”
The screen keeps showing the current photo and quietly catches up when the signal returns — it doesn’t go blank.
